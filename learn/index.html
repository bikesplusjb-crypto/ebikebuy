Learn about
your e-bike

Plain-English explanations of the parts that make an e-bike work, what goes wrong with them, and what's safe to check yourself.

    Motors and drive

    • Hub motorA motor built into the center of the front or rear wheel.
    • Mid-drive motorA motor mounted at the pedals, in the middle of the frame.
    • Motor controllerThe electronics that decide how much power goes from the battery to the motor.
    • Pedal assistThe motor helping you while you pedal.
    • ThrottleA lever or twist grip that runs the motor without pedaling.
    • Brake motor cutoffSwitches in the brake levers that turn the motor off while you brake.
    • Watts and power ratingHow much power the motor puts out, in watts.
    • Torque (Nm)How strongly the motor can twist the crank or wheel, in newton-meters.

    Displays and sensors

    • Torque sensorA sensor that measures how hard you push on the pedals.
    • Cadence sensorA sensor that detects whether, and how fast, the pedals are turning.
    • DisplayThe screen or light panel on the handlebar.
    • Error codesNumbers or letters the display shows when the system detects a problem.
    • FirmwareThe software inside the motor, display and battery.
    • Speed sensorA sensor that reads how fast the wheel turns, usually from a magnet on a spoke or the brake rotor.
    • Walk modeA setting that makes the motor push the bike slowly while you walk beside it.

    Batteries and charging

    • Lithium-ion batteryThe rechargeable battery that powers the motor, made of many small cells joined together.
    • Battery management system (BMS)The electronics inside a battery pack that protect it.
    • Voltage (V)The battery's electrical pressure, in volts.
    • Amp-hours (Ah)How much charge the battery holds, in amp-hours.
    • Watt-hours (Wh) and rangeThe total energy the battery holds: volts times amp-hours.
    • Battery chargerThe charger made for your battery's voltage, chemistry and connector.
    • Battery connectors and contactsThe metal contacts where the battery meets the bike, and the port where the charger plugs in.
    • Battery agingLithium-ion batteries slowly hold less charge as they age and get used.

    Owning an e-bike

    • Rain and waterE-bikes handle rain, but water in the wrong places causes faults.
    • Storing your batteryHow to keep a battery when you're not riding for a while.
    • E-bike classesThe three legal types of e-bike in Florida and most U.S. states.
    • Maintenance scheduleWhat to check and how often.

    Brakes, wheels and drivetrain

    • Hydraulic disc brakesBrakes that use fluid in a sealed hose to push pads onto a metal rotor.
    • Brake padsThe replaceable pads that grip the rotor.
    • Brake rotorsThe metal discs on the wheel hubs that the brake pads grip.
    • ChainThe chain that carries your pedaling (and a mid-drive's power) to the rear wheel.
    • Derailleur and gearsThe mechanism that moves the chain between gears.
    • Tire pressureHow hard the tires are pumped up.
